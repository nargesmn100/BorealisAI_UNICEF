Opening — 2 min

What is the problem?

UNICEF and national governments need to know which communities have the highest rates of child deprivation — so they can target health clinics, schools, WASH programmes, and social protection to the right places. But survey data only exists at a coarse level.

37
Nigerian states surveyed (MICS6 2021)
774
LGAs with no direct survey estimate
~40M
Children under 18 in Nigeria

The core tension

What we know (survey truth)

MICS6 gives us state-level prevalence estimates for child deprivation across 6 UNICEF dimensions: shelter, water, sanitation, nutrition, education, health.

Margin of error is acceptable at state level, but the survey was never designed for LGA or ward resolution.

What stakeholders need

Programme officers need LGA-level rankings to allocate budgets, plan outreach routes, and report to donors.

Running a full MICS at LGA resolution costs millions of dollars and takes years. We need a faster, cheaper path.

Key ask to stakeholders

Can we use satellite data, mobility signals, and administrative datasets — things that exist at very fine resolution — to disaggregate state survey numbers down to Local Government Area and grid-cell level? And can we do it in a way that is transparent, honest about uncertainty, and reproducible across countries?

Talking points

  • MICS6 is gold-standard — we never replace it; we disaggregate from it.
  • The 37-state totals are always preserved (reconciliation).
  • This is a research tool. It informs targeting, not policy by itself.
  • Nigeria is the pilot. The pipeline is designed to run on any country with MICS data.
How we solve it — 5 min

The Approach

We treat the MICS state estimates as weak supervision labels. We then train a regression model that, given satellite and administrative signals for every ~2.4 km cell in Nigeria, predicts the expected child deprivation rate — constrained to be consistent with the survey at the state level.

Technical deep dive — 5 min

How the pipeline works

The pipeline runs end-to-end with a single command: python main.py --country nga. Five sequential steps; cached parquet files at each stage.

1

Build Grid step01_build_grid.py

Overlay the Relative Wealth Index point grid on Nigeria's GADM boundaries → 103,424 cells, each ~2.4 km apart.

Output: nga_base_grid.parquet
2

Sample Proxies step02_sample_proxies.py

Sample raster values (nightlights, rainfall, GHSL built fraction, travel time) at each cell centroid. Snap OSM schools and health facilities to nearest cell.

Output: nga_grid_proxies.parquet
3

Assign Admin step03_assign_admin.py

Spatially join each cell to state, LGA, geopolitical zone, and urban/rural classification via GADM polygons.

Output: nga_grid_admin.parquet
4

Prepare Targets step04_prepare_targets.py

Compute MICS6-derived deprivation rates per state. Prioritise DHS 2018 HAZ data for nutrition. Merge NBS MPI and NEMIS D1 features into the modeling table.

Output: nga_targets.csv + nga_modeling_table.parquet
5

Train & Evaluate ridge_model.py → eval

Train Ridge regression with DHS cluster auxiliary loss. Reconcile predictions within each state to match MICS totals. Evaluate via LOZO CV, hierarchical CV, and external DHS cluster comparison.

Output: nga_predictions.parquet + all eval CSVs

Key technical choices

ChoiceWhat & Why
Ridge RegressionLinear model — fully interpretable. Each cell's score = weighted sum of 38 features. Coefficients tell you exactly which signals drive poverty up or down.
Weak supervisionState MICS targets are "soft labels." The model learns spatial patterns within states, not just between them.
DHS Auxiliary Loss~1,400 DHS GPS clusters provide finer-grained supervision. Stacked into Ridge as an additional block of auxiliary targets.
ReconciliationAfter prediction, each state's population-weighted mean is scaled to exactly match the MICS survey value. LGA values are not individually constrained.
Leakage removal8 MICS-derived survey rates (e.g. school_attendance_rate, anc_rate) were removed as predictors — they would have directly leaked the target labels, inflating apparent accuracy.
HAZ nutritionDHS 2018 Children's Recode (hw70) provides Height-for-Age Z-scores — a clinically validated stunting measure — instead of a less direct MICS proxy.

Do dimension scores sum to the composite?

No. The composite MICS target counts a child as deprived if they are deprived in ≥ 2 of 6 dimensions — not a simple sum (children can be multiply deprived). Adding 8 dimension prevalences together double-counts children deprived across multiple dimensions. Treat the composite map as the headline figure; dimension maps are thematic deep dives.

Anticipated questions + answers

Q: Why is Ridge r only 0.40?
We deliberately removed 8 leaky features — MICS-derived rates that directly overlap the deprivation target. Before removal, r was ~0.55 but those features made the model circular. 0.40 is the honest number. External DHS cluster validation (r = 0.58) confirms the spatial signal is real.
Q: How do you know the LGA predictions are right?
We can't validate at LGA level directly — no ground truth exists. What we can do: (1) state totals are exact by construction, (2) LOZO validation shows 9.1 pp median error on held-out states, (3) DHS cluster check gives ρ = 0.60 on independent data. LGA predictions are model estimates; they should inform targeting, not replace measurement.
Q: Can we do this for other countries?
Yes. The pipeline requires a MICS SPSS file, a country GADM boundary file, and a Relative Wealth Index grid. We already have a template config. The bottleneck is obtaining MICS microdata for a second country.
Q: Do the 8 dimension scores add up to the composite?
No — the composite counts children deprived in ≥ 2 dimensions. Adding 8 dimension prevalences double-counts multiply-deprived children. Dimension maps are thematic deep dives; the composite map is the headline metric consistent with UNICEF methodology.
Q: What does "reconciliation" mean and can we trust it?
After fitting, we scale each state's cell predictions so the population-weighted mean exactly matches MICS. This preserves survey truth at state level. It does not guarantee cell accuracy — the spatial pattern within a state is the model's contribution. Use LOZO and DHS cluster metrics to evaluate that spatial pattern.
Q: On the predictions map, how do I know what area a "cell" covers?
Each marker is centred on one grid sample point (Relative Wealth Index lattice), roughly every ~2.4 km. There is no drawn polygon for that cell on the HTML map. The coloured dot is a fixed pixel size for clicking, not a footprint on the ground. Use the scale bar and spacing between neighbouring dots to interpret resolution. For explicit areas with borders, use the LGA polygon map and nga_lga_predictions.csv.
Q: How long does the pipeline take to re-run?
With cached intermediates (parquet files), a full ridge-only run takes under 10 minutes: python main.py --country nga --skip-gbm --skip-gam --skip-wsnn. A full multi-model run including GBM takes ~30–60 minutes depending on machine.
